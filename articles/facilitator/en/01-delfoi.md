# What is the Delphi method

Delphi is a structured expert method for exploring the future, assessing uncertain questions, and developing well-reasoned views when unambiguous data is not available. Its core is **iteration**: the same set of questions is addressed over several rounds, and each round builds on the previous round's results and reasoning.

## Three defining features

**Anonymity.** Panelists do not know one another's identities. This reduces the influence of authority and group pressure — the argument carries weight, not who makes it.

**Iteration and feedback.** Between rounds, panelists see the group's distribution and anonymized reasoning, and may refine their position. The goal is not to force unanimity but to let views develop through argumentation.

**Managed communication.** The facilitator steers the process: frames the theses, decides on rounds, and compiles the feedback. In Pronoia this is supported by round phases, a pause between rounds, and the inter-round assistant PIRE.

## Where Delphi fits

Delphi is strongest with questions that are **multi-interpretable, value-laden, or far-future** — for example regional development, technology impacts, or policy options. It does not replace hard data where data exists; it produces a structured expert view where data is missing.

## In Pronoia

Pronoia is a facilitation platform for the Delphi process: designing the phenomenon and theses, assembling the panel, running rounds, dialogue, and reporting. Deep interpretive analysis (theming, scenarios, argument and discourse analysis) is done in the separate DAE ecosystem — Pronoia produces the structured source material for it.
