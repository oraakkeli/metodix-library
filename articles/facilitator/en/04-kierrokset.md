# Rounds and the pause

The power of Delphi lies in rounds: the same set of questions is addressed more than once, and views are allowed to develop through argumentation. Pronoia supports two ways to move from one round to the next.

## Two round models

**Re-rate.** The same theses are rated again. The panelist sees the group distribution and reasoning and can refine their answer. The answer chain (revisions) is stored, so the R1→R2 round comparison shows how views moved.

**True round Delphi.** For the next round you create **new theses** derived from the previous round's results. The round is a new questionnaire that you can build by copying the previous one as a starting point and editing it.

## The pause between rounds

You do **not** go straight from one round to the next. In between there is a pause where the facilitator considers and builds the next round — and can communicate it to the panel. This is methodologically essential: the next round's questions are derived from the previous results; don't rush.

In Pronoia the pause is its own state (`round_closed`). During it:

- The panelist sees a "the study is paused" notice and can review the previous round's material. When the round opens, the notice disappears.
- The facilitator gets a "Build round" panel: copy the previous as a base, edit the theses, and open the round. The PIRE inter-round assistant helps.

## Tip

Don't open the next round until you have reviewed the previous results and decided what to ask and why. The pause is a moment of design, not just a delay.
