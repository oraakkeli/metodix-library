# The ecosystem helps you draw a futures map

## Three phases

The Metodix Delphi ecosystem consists of three phases that connect to one another, though each can also be used independently. The planning tools (Delphi Planning Ecosystem) are used to build a Delphi design; data collection is carried out with the Pronoia application; and the results are transferred to the third-phase ecosystem (Delphi Analysis Ecosystem) for analysis. Two methods that can also be used on their own are integrated into the planning phase: the Futures Wheel and the Relevance Tree. In the analysis phase, a range of analysis techniques is available beyond traditional quantitative and qualitative methods: argument and dialogue analysis, CLA and MLP layered analyses (Causal Layered Analysis, Multi-Level Perspective), weak signals, tension analysis, and scenario building along several alternative models.

Claude AI is used in planning and analysis, and the services can be shared for independent use via plugin technology, either as a complete package or as separate modules. Pronoia is a web application at [www.pronoia.fi](http://www.pronoia.fi). It receives the planning data so that the Delphi panel is ready for execution — the facilitator can of course still edit the survey, the panel and the process. Instead of advance preparation, a Delphi study can also be run in Pronoia without any input data, either without AI or with its help.

## The AI assistants Kastalia and Kassotis

The ecosystem offers two AI assistants, Kastalia and Kassotis, christened in the spirit of the Delphic tradition of antiquity. The name of the Pronoia application springs from the same story. The researcher is cast in the role of the oracle, Pythia.

On the slopes of Mount Parnassus, at Delphi, asking the future was a process. No one walked straight up to the oracle. A traveller first met the sanctuary of Athena Pronaia, which preceded the main temple. In antiquity the sanctuary's name was written in two ways: Pronaia, "she who stands before the temple", and Pronoia, "she who sees ahead, the far-sighted". Both readings hold true in this ecosystem as well. Pronoia is a place one passes through before understanding is born. It does not predict the future, but it sets in motion a journey on which knowledge about the future is weighed alone and together.

On the journey "to the future" there are two springs, and the assistants take their names from them.

Kastalia — the Castalian spring of the classical sources — rises outside the sanctuary, by the entrance. There everyone purified themselves: the questioners, the priesthood and Pythia herself. Kastalia predicts nothing; she makes sure that nothing impure or unclear is carried into the temple. Kastalia is the assistant of the threshold, and she walks beside the researcher through the first two phases.

In planning (Delphi Planning Ecosystem), Kastalia helps clarify what is being asked and of whom: the phenomenon description, the research orientation, the composition of the panel and the wording of the theses. On the temple wall at Delphi it reads "Nothing in excess". Kastalia sees to it that every thesis is sharp enough to lead the panel into argument.

In Pronoia, Kastalia moves to the gate to receive the panelists. She has a preparatory conversation (the chat stage) with each of them before the actual Delphi round. In this way she makes sure that everyone enters the dialogue understanding the question, and can do so in their own words and in their own language. She also takes care that the collected material is valid and intact when it is time to step deeper.

Kassotis — Cassotis in the classical sources — is the second spring, whose water flows inside the temple. According to Pausanias (the second-century describer of Delphi), the water sank underground and flowed into the adyton, the innermost chamber, where only Pythia could drink from it. Kassotis is the researcher's second assistant, and you meet her in the third phase, analysis (Delphi Analysis Ecosystem). Kassotis opens up the choices and comments produced by the panel, round by round and layer by layer: arguments, dialogue moves, discourses, deep structures, tensions and weak signals. Kassotis does her work tirelessly, from one method to the next, one quality gate at a time. But note the division of labour, inherited directly from Delphi: Kassotis is running water, not a voice. She feeds interpretation, but never utters a prophecy. Synthesis, conclusions and the final word belong to the researcher. At ancient Delphi the god gave the signs, but it was the human who created the meaning.

And perhaps the finest detail of the story: both springs draw their water from the same groundwater flow of Mount Parnassus. One base of knowledge, two springs. One rises to the surface at the gate for all to see; the other appears in the innermost chamber, for the researcher alone. Kastalia prepares, Kassotis deepens; the researcher sees and understands!

## The futures map

Through these two roles, AI in the Metodix ecosystems is a sovereign master of futures research methods, a diligent assistant researcher and a technically versatile colleague. At times it presents its proposals, analyses and theories with too much confidence. It can hold a course once the course has been set. If, alongside the analogy from antiquity, we take up a map-and-navigation metaphor, AI offers a compass. The second instrument it offers is even more important: it can draw futures maps, with which you can navigate the future in the imagination.

A compass shows directions; a map outlines the terrain. The limitation of a futures map is that its validity cannot be verified by comparing it with reality. It is only a model or a hypothesis — but one that improves when it is put to the test. That is what the methods of futures research are for. A futures map is also layered, plural and multi-path. It is best represented by the central way of depicting the future: scenario building.

A futures map improves through asking, gathering knowledge, conceptualising, questioning and discussing. All of this is worth practising with the assisting AI roles as well. They draw from their sources proposals that already look finished but rarely, if ever, are. In the Socratic manner, you get more out of your AI colleagues by engaging them in conversation and even in debate. If you do not, the map does not improve and the navigator's skills do not grow. It is the researcher's task to take responsibility for the choice of route. Kastalia and Kassotis have been trained to do their part and to seek opposing views to their own proposals. Those are worth taking up, even when they lead to detours from the main route. The assistants make sure that no one gets lost between the checkpoints.
