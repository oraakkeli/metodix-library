# How do I answer?

Welcome to the panel! You were invited because your view matters. Answering is easy, and you can do it at your own pace.

## Two parts to each thesis

**1. A rating.** For most theses you pick on a scale how likely or how desirable you consider the statement. There is no right or wrong answer — this is your assessment.

**2. A reason.** The most important part is a short reason: *why* you rated it that way. The reasoning is what makes the panel valuable — it helps others understand your perspective and develop their own thinking. A few sentences is enough.

## Other ways to answer

Besides the scale you may meet other question types:

- **Ranking and multiple choice.** Put the options in order of importance, or pick the ones that apply.
- **Time window.** You estimate *when* something will happen: give the earliest and latest year, either by dragging the two handles on the line or by typing the years into the fields. A narrow window signals a confident stance, a wide one an uncertain stance — both are equally valuable. If you do not believe it will happen at all, choose "will never happen" (when offered) — do not force yourself to invent a year.
- **Time series.** You see the realised development as a curve and continue it with your own estimates: drag the orange points into place on the chart or type the numbers into the fields. Estimate the direction and the order of magnitude — there is no exact "right" number.

## Practical tips

- You can save your answers one at a time or all at once — even partial answers are saved.
- You can come back and edit your answer before the round closes.
- If a thesis is unclear, say so in your reason — that is valuable feedback too.

No rush: a considered reason is better than a quick guess.
