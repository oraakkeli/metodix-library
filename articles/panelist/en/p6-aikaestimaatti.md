# How do I answer a timing estimate?

Some theses don't ask *how likely* something is, but **when** it will happen. You're asked to give the year by which you think the thesis becomes true.

## How to answer

- **Type a year** within the given range (e.g. 2025–2050). It's your best estimate of when the statement will be true.
- If you believe it won't happen within this window — or never — choose **"Not within this horizon / never"**.

There's no right answer: it's your judgment about timing.

## Rationale

As with other theses, the most valuable part is a **short rationale**: why that year? What would have to happen first for your estimate to hold? Rationales help others understand what your timing rests on.

## Rounds

In the next round you'll see the panel's **timing distribution** — the median year and how estimates spread. You may reconsider your own; converging on timing across rounds is at the heart of the Delphi method.

> Note: some studies also use a **time window**, where you give two years (earliest–latest) — there the width of your range expresses your uncertainty.
