# Anonymity and disagreement

Two things that make this method different — and worth knowing so you can take part freely.

## You are anonymous

Other panelists cannot see your identity. You are known only by a neutral code. This is deliberate: it ensures the **argument carries weight, not who makes it**. You can say honestly what you think without your position, title, or familiarity affecting how your view is received.

## Disagreement is valuable

The goal of this panel is **not always unanimity**. Some questions seek a shared estimate, but others — especially those about values and desirability — depend on **different reasoned views becoming visible**.

So you are not "wrong" if you disagree with the majority. A reasoned minority view can be the study's most important contribution. Don't smooth over your position to fit in — say what you really think and why.

## In short

- Say honestly what you think — you are anonymous.
- Always give a short reason — that is most valuable.
- You may disagree — reasoned disagreement is valuable, not a problem.
